Working offline

Relay keeps your issues on your computer, so it works the same on a plane as at your desk. What you change offline waits and reaches Jira when you're back (Offline and sync).

What Relay keeps by itself​

Without you asking, Relay keeps:

  • Your lists: My work, Inbox and the issues your rules look at, refreshed in the background.
  • Each issue you open: its description, comments, links and development.
  • Your logged time, week by week, for the weeks you look at on the Timesheet.
  • The images and videos you view, while Cache attachments is on (it is unless you turn it off).

Lists bring each issue's status, assignee and other fields, but not its description or comments: those come when you open the issue. So an issue you've never opened has its row, but not its text.

Before you go: Fetch Everything​

Fetch Everything reads the rest at once, like downloading a map for offline use. Run it from ⌘K, or from My work's View menu › Sync › Fetch Everything.

It reads, in turn:

  1. Epics: the issues of each epic in your sidebar.
  2. Issues: the description and every comment of each issue you're involved in, Inbox's and those epics'.
  3. Links: each issue's links and development (branches, commits, pull requests).
  4. Images: the image attachments on those issues, and a video's preview picture (not the video).
  5. Avatars: the pictures of the people on them.

While it runs, the title bar reads Syncing. Click it to see how far along each step is, and Stop to end it after the request under way. What was read stays.

If the connection goes while it runs, it stops and says where. What it couldn't read (an issue Jira won't give you) is listed under Sync failed in the title bar, and the rest goes on. Each step also runs on its own from ⌘K: Fetch Epics, Fetch Issues, Fetch Links, Fetch Images and Fetch Avatars.

Images follow your attachment settings: with Cache attachments off, none are kept, and the cache's size limit still applies (see Where it's kept).

Getting ready for a trip:

  1. Run Fetch Everything while you're online, and wait until the title bar says Synced.
  2. Look at the Timesheet weeks you'll need, so the time logged there is on your computer too.
  3. Open any issue you'll need that isn't yours or in your Inbox; opening it keeps it.

What works offline​

  • Reading: lists, boards, epics, issues with their descriptions and comments, the Timesheet and Activity.
  • Finding: ⌘P and ⌘K search everything Relay keeps, search operators included.
  • Changing issues: status, assignee, priority, fields, the description and links. A status can change offline when Relay knows where the issue can move, which it learns when you open the issue online.
  • Comments and files: comment, reply, edit, and attach files.
  • Time: log time, and change or delete what you logged.
  • New issues: create them and publish them; they're sent when you're back.
  • Undo: ⌘Z takes back any change not sent yet.
  • Rules and Inbox: your rules run on your computer, on what Relay has.

What needs Jira​

  • Searching Jira itself: ⌘P's results from Jira, and looking up an issue by key that Relay doesn't have.
  • A second status change on the same issue: Jira has to take the first before it says where the issue can go next.
  • Choices Relay hasn't read yet, such as a project's sprints, versions or priorities: the menu says they load once you're online.
  • Files Relay doesn't show: PDFs, documents and other files open in Jira, in your browser.
  • Images you haven't viewed: an image in a description or comment that isn't kept shows as its name, saying it isn't available offline.
  • Refreshing: ⌘R, Refresh Everything and Refresh issue … from Jira need a connection.

Files​

An issue's files are listed under its description, under Attachments, each as a tile with its name, size and age. Images show a picture; other files show an icon. Images in a description or comment show inline, where they were put.

Opening. Click a tile. Images open in Relay's viewer, where you can zoom and move around, and videos play there. Other files open in Jira, in your browser. Right-click an image (PNG or JPEG) for Copy Image.

Attaching to the issue. Any of:

  • the paperclip in the issue's header, or ⇧⌘U;
  • Attach a file to … in ⌘K;
  • drop files on the issue.

Adding to a comment. The paperclip in the comment box (⌥⌘U) puts a file in what you're writing: an image shows in the text, another file by its name. Pasting or dropping a file into the text does the same. The file is attached when you post the comment; take it out of the text before posting, and it never is.

Removing. Right-click a tile and choose Remove file. ⌘Z brings it back while the removal still waits to be sent; once Jira has removed it, it's gone.

Offline. Files you attach are kept on your computer and wait with your other changes; they upload when you're back online, before the comment that shows them. Until then, Relay shows them from your computer. Each file can be up to 100 MB.

Where it's kept​

Everything is in your account's folder on this computer. Settings › Data & privacy shows it:

  • Local database: its size, and Show in Finder.
  • Cache attachments: keeps the images and videos you view, for offline. Turning it off empties the cache.
  • Attachment cache limit: 250 MB to 2 GB (500 MB at first). The files viewed longest ago go first.
  • Cached attachments: how much is kept, and Clear older than 7 days, Clear over 5 MB or Clear all.
  • Clear data: pick what to clear. Copies of Jira data (issues, descriptions and comments, images and avatars, worklogs) download again; what's only yours is gone for good. Changes waiting for Jira are never cleared.

Settings › Account & sync › Keep history for also decides how long done issues, activity and images you haven't viewed are kept.
