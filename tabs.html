Tabs: keep your active issues open

An issue you open gets a tab: it stays in the sidebar, under Open, until you close it. So the handful of issues you're working on are always one click away, and they're still there tomorrow.

Opening an issue in a tab​

  • Double-click a row in My work or an epic, or press ↵ on it.
  • In ⌘P, press ↵ on an issue. ⌘T opens it in a tab in the background, so you can open several and stay in ⌘P.
  • Open in tab in any issue's menu.
  • ⌘T opens the issue you're looking at in a list's panel (a single click in My work shows it there, without a tab).

Opening an issue that already has a tab switches to it.

The Open section​

The sidebar's Open section lists your tabs, with how many there are.

Grouped by epic. A new tab joins its epic's group, made when needed with the epic's name and colour. Issues without an epic sit on their own. You can turn this off with Group tabs by epic in Settings › Appearance, in a group's menu, or in ⌘K.

Folding. Click a group's row to fold it, and again to unfold it. Click Open or Pinned to fold the whole section.

Your own groups. Right-click a tab for Add to new group or Add to "…" an existing one, and Remove from group. Or drag a tab: onto the middle of another tab to put them together, beside a tab to move it, onto Open to take it out of its group. A tab you place yourself stays where you put it.

A group's menu (right-click its row) has Rename and colour…, New tab in group… (⌘P, opening into this group), Collapse group, Open all links in browser, Ungroup (its tabs stay open) and Close group (its tabs close).

Switching between tabs​

  • Click a tab in the sidebar.
  • ⇧⌘] and ⇧⌘[ go to the next and previous tab, pinned ones first, round from the last to the first.
  • ⌘P lists your recent issues first: a dot marks one open in a tab, a star one that's pinned.

⌘[ and ⌘] go back and forward through the pages you've visited, tabs included.

Closing tabs​

  • ⌥⌘W closes the tab you're looking at. (⌘W closes the window, as in any Mac app.)
  • The × on a tab's row, when you point at it.
  • A tab's menu: Close, Close other tabs, Close all tabs.
  • Close all, beside the Open heading.

Close other tabs and Close all leave your pinned tabs open. Closing the tab you're on takes you back to the page you were on before it.

Closed one by mistake? Press ⌘Z. Closing is a step you can undo, like the toast says ("Closed NW-118 · Undo"), and the tab comes back where it was. The same goes for opening a tab, pinning, grouping and moving: each is an undo step. Folding a group isn't.

Typing in a tab's menu finds its entries, as in any menu: close lists all three.

Pinned issues​

Pin the issues you always come back to: a recurring task, the release checklist, your epic. A pinned issue moves to the Pinned section at the top of the sidebar, above Open, and stays there:

  • Close other tabs and Close all leave it open.
  • It leaves its group while pinned. Unpinned, it goes back to the end of Open.

To pin or unpin:

  • the star in the issue's header (Pin / Unpin);
  • ⌥⌘P on the issue you're looking at, or on the issue lit in ⌘P;
  • Pin in a tab's menu;
  • drag a tab onto Pinned.

Pinning an issue that has no tab opens one.

Find a pinned issue. In ⌘P, turn on the Pinned filter (or In tabs for every open tab) and type: only those issues match. Type your project's key, such as NW, to see them all.

Open issues in preview​

If you open many issues just to glance at them, turn on Open issues in preview in Settings › Appearance (it's off by default). Then an opened issue shows without a tab, and the next one you open takes its place, as in a code editor's preview.

To keep the one you're looking at, click Open in tab in its header or press ⌘T. Pinning it keeps it too, and ⌥⌘W closes it. A preview isn't in the sidebar and isn't an undo step. Turning the setting off turns the preview into a tab.

After a restart​

Your tabs, their groups, your pins and your recent issues (the last 50) are kept on your computer. Quit and come back, and they're all there. With Open to: Where you left off (Settings › General, the default), Relay also opens on the page you were on, with Back and Forward as you left them.

The undo history isn't kept: after a restart, ⌘Z starts fresh.
