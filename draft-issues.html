Draft issues: notes and plans of your own

A draft issue is an issue that lives only on your computer. It has a key of its own (D-1, D-2…), a page like any issue's, and nothing of it reaches Jira until you publish it. Nobody else sees it, it doesn't notify anyone, and it stays across restarts.

These are drafts of issues. The dashed worklogs on the Timesheet are something else: see Drafts under Time tracking.

What they're for​

Notes to yourself. In a meeting, someone mentions that the empty price list says the wrong thing. Save a draft, "Ask design about the empty price list copy", and get back to the meeting. It waits in My work, and nobody gets an email about a half-thought.

Planning a feature. Before you share a plan, write it as drafts under its epic: "Price history for each product", "Bulk stock update from a CSV", "Spike: pick a currency rates provider". Give each a description, move them between statuses, link them ("D-2 is blocked by D-3"), and publish them when the plan is ready.

Small things. A typo you noticed, a test that's flaky now and then, a log line that's too loud. Not worth a Jira issue yet, but worth not forgetting. Keep them as drafts, and discard the ones that turn out not to matter.

When it becomes real, publish it: it goes to Jira as a new issue, with what you wrote on it.

Making one​

Open New issue: ⌘N (Ctrl+N on Windows and Linux), the New issue button in My work's header, or the one on an epic's page, which puts the issue in that epic. In ⌘K it's New Issue….

Pick the project, the type and the epic, give it a title and, if you like, a description. Then:

  • Save as draft (⇧⌘↵) keeps it on your computer as a draft.
  • Create (⌘↵) sends it to Jira as a new issue.

The draft opens on its own page.

Where drafts show​

  • My work: every draft, in a Drafts group of its own, first.
  • An epic's page: its drafts, first, in a Drafts group of their own, whatever the filters or grouping.

What a draft can hold​

A draft's page is any issue's page, and almost everything on it works:

  • its title and description, rich text and all;
  • its fields: status, assignee, priority, epic, labels and the rest;
  • links to other issues, drafts included;
  • comments, and files attached to it or to a comment.

Each change is made at once and can be undone with ⌘Z, offline too. None of it goes to Jira, and none of it is counted in the title bar's waiting changes.

Publishing​

When a draft is ready, publish it:

  • Publish in the draft's header;
  • Publish draft in its right-click menu;
  • Publish Draft in ⌘K, with the draft open or selected. Select several drafts in a list and it publishes each of them.

What happens:

  1. The draft leaves Drafts and becomes a new issue on its way to Jira.
  2. Jira creates it with its title, type, epic, assignee and the other fields you set, all at once, so watchers see one "created".
  3. The rest follows, in the order you did it: changes to its description, its files, its comments, a move to another status.
  4. It takes its Jira key (NW-222, say). Its tab follows it to the new key, and, published from the draft's page, that page opens once Jira has it.

If Jira refuses it (a field it requires, say), it stays your draft, on its page, and Relay says why: fix it and publish again.

Publishing works offline too: the issue opens as a new issue, waits with your other changes and goes when you're back online, taking its Jira key then (Offline and sync). Publishing can't be undone with ⌘Z; while the new issue still waits for Jira, you can discard it from the list of waiting changes.

Discarding​

Discard a draft with the trash button in its header, Discard draft in its right-click menu, or Discard Draft in ⌘K. The draft goes, with its comments, files and links.

Changed your mind? ⌘Z brings it back, with everything on it, and opens it again.

A draft or a new issue?​

Both are made with the same form, and differ in when they go:

  • A draft (Save as draft, key D-1) waits for you. It goes to Jira only when you publish it.
  • A new issue (Create) goes to Jira straight away. Made offline, it shows New for its key until Jira has it and gives it a real one; it's already on its way, waiting with your other changes.

From an AI app​

An AI app connected to Relay's MCP server can save drafts too, so it can suggest several issues from a conversation for you to read first. Making a draft needs no approval, since nothing leaves your computer; publishing one is a change to Jira, so it needs the server's read-write mode.
