Lists, boards and filters

My work, Triage and a project's page are the same page over different issues, so they work the same way: a list or a board, the View menu, the filter bar, the keys and the right panel. An epic's page shares all of it too.

  • My work (⌘2): the issues assigned to you.
  • Triage (⌘8): new work in the projects you follow, unassigned and in no epic. An issue leaves it as soon as it's assigned or put in an epic.
  • A project's page: click the project's row under Projects in the sidebar. It shows that project's Triage issues.

Each view keeps its own layout, grouping, sorting and filters.

The list​

The list groups your issues by status, in workflow order: To Do, then In Progress, In Review and Done, each with its count. Each row shows the issue's status, type, key and summary, its epic, and when it last changed. A group folds with the arrow beside its name. On My work, draft issues you haven't published come first, in a Drafts group of their own.

Click a row, or move to it with ↑ ↓, and the issue opens in the right panel: its status, assignee, priority and epic, its description, and its comments, activity and worklogs, where you can work on it without leaving the list.

The board​

The board shows the same issues as columns, one per status.

Switch between them with the List | Board switch at the top right, or the keys: ⌥⌘L for the list, ⌥⌘B for the board. In ⌘K they're Show as List and Show as Board. Each view remembers which one you last used.

Drag a card to another column to move the issue to that status, or press ⇧→ / ⇧← to move the card under the cursor one column. Relay moves it the way Jira's workflow allows:

  • When the workflow leads to exactly one of the column's statuses, the issue moves there.
  • When it leads to several, the status menu opens where you dropped the card, with just those, so you pick.
  • When there's no way there from the issue's status, a message says so and nothing changes.

While you drag, the columns the issue can't move to are dimmed. A move is saved on your computer first and sent to Jira, like any change, and ⌘Z undoes it. Cards drag only while the board is grouped by status or status group.

The View menu​

View, beside the switch, holds how the issues are shown. Each submenu names what's chosen.

  • Grouping: Status (a group or column per Jira status), Status Group (To do, In progress, Blocked, In review and Done, each holding the statuses that belong to it), Epic, Parent, Project, or None. Triage starts grouped by project when its issues span several projects.
  • Ordering: Updated, Created, Status or Key, then the direction in its own words (Newest First, Oldest First; Workflow Order; Lowest Key First). ⇧⌘O reverses it.
  • Empty Status Columns: on, the board has a column for every status of your issues' projects, empty ones too, so you can drag a card to any of them. Off, only statuses with issues get a column.
  • Columns: which of those empty columns show, one checkbox per status, and Show All. A status with issues always shows.
  • Sync: Refresh (⌘R) reads the list from Jira again; Fetch Everything reads everything Relay keeps from Jira again (see Offline and sync).
  • Reset returns this view to the defaults; Set as Default makes its options the default for every view without options of its own.

The filter bar​

The bar under the header narrows the issues. Click Filter (⇧⌘F) to open the filter menu. Each field opens a submenu of its values, each with how many issues have it:

  • Project, Status, Type, Priority and Reporter.
  • Links: blocked by an open issue, blocks an open issue, has issue links, no issue links.
  • Updated and Created: in the last day, 7 days or 30 days (Updated also offers "Not in 30 days").
  • Due: overdue, in the next 7 or 30 days, or no due date.
  • Pull request: has one, or doesn't.

Pick a value to add it; pick it again to take it away. Any clears that field. Type while the menu is open to find a value in any field.

Combining filters. Several values in one field match any of them (Status: To Do or In Progress). Different fields must all match (Priority: High, and Type: Bug). Each filter shows as a pill, such as Priority: High: click it to change it, or its × to remove it. The count on the right says how many issues show, as in 2 of 8 issues, and Clear removes every filter at once.

Filters are kept per view: My work, Triage, each project and each epic keep their own, also after you quit and reopen Relay. Each change is a step you can undo with ⌘Z.

Keyboard​

Every issue list and board works the same way:

  • ↑ ↓ move, and show the issue in the right panel. On a board, ← → move between columns.
  • ↵ opens the issue in a tab; Space peeks at it in the right panel, opening the panel if it's hidden.
  • ⌥X adds the issue under the cursor to the selection, or takes it out. ⌘A selects everything; Esc clears the selection.
  • ⌥S, ⌥A and ⌥P change its status, assignee or priority; ⌥B links an issue that blocks it, ⌥L links another.
  • ⌘↵ opens the selection in the browser; ⌘C copies its keys, ⇧⌘C its links.
  • ⇧F10, or a right-click, opens the issue's menu.

The key strip under the list shows these keys as you work. ⌘/ hides or shows it. Every key is listed in Keyboard shortcuts.

The right panel​

The right panel shows the issue selected in the list or board, and follows the selection. ⌥⌘\ hides or shows it, for example to see every column of a wide board.

Development: branches, pull requests and checks​

When your Jira is connected to GitHub or Bitbucket, an issue's rail has a Development section: the branches, pull requests and commits that name the issue's key, and how their checks went. An open pull request shows its review (Approved, Changes requested, In review), a closed one how it closed. Click a row to open it in your browser.

An epic's page shows it on each of its rows: the pull request's number beside the summary (#44). The filter bar's Pull request filter keeps only the issues that have one, or only those that don't. Like everything else, it's kept on your computer, so it shows offline too.
