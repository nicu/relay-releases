Epics

An epic's page answers "where is this at?" in one look: how much is done, what's in progress, who's on it, and whether it'll make its target. It's one click from the sidebar, and it works offline once Relay has read it.

Finding an epic​

  • The sidebar. Under Projects, each project you follow folds open to its open epics, and to the epics your own issues are in. The number beside an epic is how many of your open issues it holds. Click an epic to show its page.
  • ⌘P. Type the epic's key or a word of its name, as for any issue (see Go to issue).
  • From one of its issues. The epic in the issue's breadcrumb opens the epic's page.

Projects and epics in the sidebar can be turned off in Settings › Appearance › Show projects and epics in the sidebar.

The head​

The top of the page says what the epic is and how far along:

  • its title and summary (the first paragraph of its description);
  • its status, its lead (the epic's assignee), its target (its due date) and how many issues it has;
  • a progress bar split by status: done, in review, in progress, blocked and to do, with done's share as a percentage and each part's figure under it. When any of its issues has story points, the bar counts points; otherwise it counts issues.

The head scrolls away as you go down the issues, and the breadcrumb stays.

Its issues: list, board or timeline​

Below the head are all of the epic's issues, not only yours. The switch at the end of the bar over them shows them as a list (⌥⌘L), a board (⌥⌘B) or a timeline (⌥⌘T). The page remembers which you chose.

The list and the board work as on My work (see Lists, boards and filters): the same rows and cards, keys, selection and menus. Dragging a card to another column moves the issue to that status, the way Jira's workflow allows, and ⌘Z undoes it.

On an epic's page, View › Grouping offers Status, Status Group and None: its issues already share their epic.

The timeline​

The timeline puts the epic on a calendar, in weeks.

Progress (the burn-up) draws two lines week by week: scope, the epic's issues by when they were created, and done, by when they were resolved. Today is a faint vertical line, and the target (the epic's due date) a dashed red one; past the target, the chart is hatched red. Hover over the chart to read any week's figures.

At this pace says when the work would be done, from how fast issues were finished over the last 4 weeks, projected from today until it meets scope: for example "At this pace: 24 Nov (18 Nov – 2 Dec)". The range in brackets comes from the slowest and the fastest of those weeks, and is drawn as a faint band ahead of today. It gives a date only when at least 3 issues were done in those weeks; otherwise it says there's too little done lately, nothing done in the last 4 weeks, or All done. Hover over it to see what it's based on.

Under the chart, each issue has a bar from its start date (else its sprint's start, else when it was created) to its due date (else its sprint's end, else when it was resolved, else today), coloured by its status. The rows follow the list's order and grouping, and select, open and take keys as the list's do.

Zooming. Pinch on a trackpad, or ⌘-scroll, to zoom around the pointer; ⌥⌘= and ⌥⌘− zoom in and out, and ⌥⌘0 fits the whole timeline again. Zoomed in, scroll sideways (or ⇧-scroll) to move through the weeks.

Filtering an epic​

The bar over the issues is the same filter bar as on My work, with one more pill: Assignee, for whose issues show.

  • ⌘F chooses people: you first, then whoever has the most issues, and Unassigned last.
  • ⌥M shows only yours.
  • Filter (⇧⌘F) adds any other field: status, type, priority, links, due date and the rest.

The list, the board and the timeline show what the filters let through; the progress bar and the head's figures stay the whole epic's. With people chosen, the pace reads At your pace (only you) or At their pace, since it counts only their issues. Each epic keeps its own filters, also after you quit Relay.

The right panel​

The right panel shows the Epic: its Releases (the fix versions of its issues, each with how many are done), its Details (Lead, Start, Target and Labels, which you can change in place), its Development and its Links.

Select an issue in the list or board and the panel shows that issue, with Epic beside it in the panel's header to switch back. ⌥⌘1 shows the epic, ⌥⌘2 the selected issue.

Planning in the epic​

New issue in the header creates an issue in the epic. To plan before anything reaches Jira, save it as a draft issue instead: drafts stay on your computer and come first on the epic's page, in a Drafts group of their own, whatever the filters, until you publish them.
